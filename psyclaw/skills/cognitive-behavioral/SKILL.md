# CBT — Cognitive Behavioral Tools

Structured skill for identifying cognitive distortions, challenging unhelpful thought patterns, and building behavioral change.

## When to Activate This Skill

- The user is stuck in a spiral of negative self-talk.
- The user is catastrophizing, mind-reading, or black-and-white thinking.
- The user wants practical tools to change patterns, not just feel heard.
- Anxiety or depression loops are present.

## Common Cognitive Distortions to Watch For

| Distortion | Example | Reframe Direction |
|---|---|---|
| **All-or-nothing thinking** | "I always fail." | Look for nuance, partial success, context |
| **Catastrophizing** | "This will ruin everything." | Examine actual probability and impact |
| **Mind-reading** | "They must think I'm an idiot." | Explore evidence for/against |
| **Overgeneralization** | "Nothing ever works out for me." | Identify specific exceptions |
| **Should statements** | "I should be stronger." | Replace with "I'd like to" / "It would help if" |
| **Personalization** | "It's my fault they're upset." | Examine actual causal role |
| **Emotional reasoning** | "I feel stupid, so I must be." | Distinguish feelings from facts |
| **Discounting positives** | "That doesn't count." | Validate genuine achievements |
| **Labeling** | "I'm a failure." | Separate behavior from identity |

## Thought Record Process (Guided)

Walk the user through these steps:

1. **Situation** — What happened? Where, when, with whom?
2. **Automatic thought** — What went through your mind? What did it mean to you?
3. **Emotion** — What emotion did you feel? Rate intensity 0–100.
4. **Evidence FOR the thought** — What facts support this thought?
5. **Evidence AGAINST the thought** — What facts challenge this thought?
6. **Balanced thought** — What's a more balanced, realistic way to see this?
7. **Outcome** — How do you feel now? Re-rate the emotion 0–100.

Offer to do this collaboratively — ask one step at a time, wait for their response.

## Behavioral Activation

When low mood reduces motivation and activity, which in turn lowers mood:

1. **Map the cycle** — Show how inactivity reinforces low mood.
2. **Identify small pleasurable or meaningful activities** — Not big goals. Tiny steps.
3. **Schedule one activity** — Specific time, specific action, low bar.
4. **Track and review** — What happened? What got in the way? Adjust.

## Between-Session Practices

Suggest one of the following based on what came up:
- **Daily thought record** — One entry per day for one week.
- **Behavioral activation log** — Rate mood before/after small activities.
- **Evidence journal** — One piece of positive evidence per day.
- **Worry time** — 15-minute scheduled window to contain rumination.

## What to Avoid

- Do not move to CBT tools before emotional validation is complete.
- Do not push the process if the person is in acute distress.
- Do not turn sessions into homework factories — one practice at a time.
